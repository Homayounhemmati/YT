San Antonio is the least expensive of the large Texas metros on the government's own
measures, and the reason is simple: its rents are below the national average. Take
the same basket of goods and services that costs about the same in Austin, add
rent about a quarter lower at HUD's benchmark, and you have the **cost of living in San
Antonio** — lower than Austin's and Houston's on the line that matters most.

## What it costs to live in San Antonio

One person in a one-bedroom apartment, spending what the average one-person US
household spends, priced at San Antonio's own price level:

| Monthly cost, one person | San Antonio |
|---|---|
| Rent and utilities (HUD Fair Market Rent, one bedroom) | $1,177 |
| Groceries | $265 |
| Eating out | $180 |
| Car purchase and fuel | $300 |
| Car insurance and upkeep | $191 |
| Health care | $323 |
| Phone service | $68 |
| Entertainment | $173 |
| Clothing | $87 |
| Education | $66 |
| Public transport | $59 |
| Personal care | $49 |
| **Total** | **$2,937** |

Rent is 40% of that month; for a family of four in a two-bedroom, whose total comes to
about $5,813, it is 25%. The two-bedroom Fair Market Rent is $1,426. Those San Antonio
living expenses are an average household's — put in your own figures in the cost of
living calculator and the total follows.

## What the indices mean

The Bureau of Economic Analysis puts the San Antonio metro area at 94.7 for 2024 on a
scale where the US average is 100 — 5.3 points below average, the lowest of the Texas
metros on this site.

- **Housing rents: 94.6.** Below average, where Austin's are 120.4 and Houston's
  104.5. This is San Antonio's advantage in one number.
- **Goods: 93.8.** Groceries, clothing and cars are cheaper than the national
  average.
- **Utilities: 82.2.** Well below average.
- **Other services: 96.2.** A little under the national figure.

One detail in BEA's data is worth knowing if you are choosing between the two cities
eighty miles apart. San Antonio's goods and other-services levels are identical to
Austin's — 93.8 and 96.2 in both. On the government's figures, groceries, eating out,
health care and a car cost the same in either place. The gap between them is housing,
and a little in utilities.

So is San Antonio expensive? No — it is below average on every component BEA
measures. The average rent in San Antonio at HUD's 40th-percentile benchmark, for
Bexar County and including tenant-paid utilities, runs from $1,077 for a studio to
$2,132 for four bedrooms.

## Texas income tax on your salary

None: Texas has no personal income tax. A single San Antonio resident on $95,000 keeps
about $75,663 after federal tax, Social Security and Medicare, an effective 20.4% —
the same as in Austin or Houston. Because the paycheck is identical across Texas, the
comparison between Texas cities is purely a comparison of costs, and on costs San
Antonio comes out ahead.

Covering the one-person month above takes a gross salary of about $41,155. A family
of four needs about $81,383 filing jointly. Both are break-even — no savings, no debt
payments — so they mark the floor for a household like the average one.

## Who San Antonio suits

- **Anyone weighing it against Austin on the same salary.** With identical tax and
  identical prices for goods and services, the whole difference is rent: $1,177
  against $1,562 for one bedroom, $1,426 against $1,852 for two.
- **Single renters.** Rent is a smaller share of a one-person budget here than in
  Austin or Houston, so living alone costs less of the month.
- **Households on one income.** The family-of-four salary floor is the lowest of the
  Texas metros on this site.

It suits less someone whose job market is concentrated in Austin: the cheaper rent
is real, but a daily commute between the two cities would add the car lines back,
and then some.

## How we calculate this

Rent is HUD's Fair Market Rent for fiscal 2026 for Bexar County, matched to HUD's own
county file. Every other line is the Bureau of Labor Statistics' 2024 average for a
household of that size, priced with San Antonio's 2024 BEA price levels: goods for
groceries, clothing and cars; other services for eating out, health care, phone and
insurance. HUD's rent includes the utilities a tenant pays, so they are not counted
twice. The salary figures come from the same engine as the Texas paycheck
calculator.
