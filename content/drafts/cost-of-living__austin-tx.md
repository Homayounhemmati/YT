Austin is a city where one line of the budget does almost all the work. On the
federal government's own measure, prices across the Austin metro area sit a little
below the national average overall — and rent sits far above it. Whether Austin is
expensive for you depends almost entirely on how much space you need, which is why
the **cost of living in Austin** is better read line by line than as a single score.

## What it costs to live in Austin

For one person in a one-bedroom apartment, spending what the average one-person US
household spends on everyday categories, priced at Austin's own price level:

| Monthly cost, one person | Austin |
|---|---|
| Rent and utilities (HUD Fair Market Rent, one bedroom) | $1,562 |
| Groceries | $278 |
| Eating out | $195 |
| Car purchase and fuel | $321 |
| Car insurance, repairs and fees | $205 |
| Public transport and fares | $66 |
| Health care and insurance | $340 |
| Clothing | $90 |
| Phone service | $69 |
| Household services | $86 |
| Household supplies | $41 |
| Furniture and household items | $122 |
| Entertainment | $188 |
| Personal care | $54 |
| Education | $71 |
| **Total** | **$3,687** |

Rent is 42% of that month. For a family of four in a two-bedroom the total is about
$7,018, and rent falls to 26% of it — the two-bedroom Fair Market Rent is $1,852
against $1,562 for one bedroom, while food and transport more than double. Austin
punishes the single renter more than the family.

Those Austin living expenses are a typical household's, not yours. Replace any line
with your own figure in the cost of living calculator and the total, and the salary
it takes, move with it.

## What the indices mean

The Bureau of Economic Analysis measures prices in every metro area against a US
average of 100. Austin's overall level for 2024 is 98.1, but the components pull hard
in opposite directions:

- **Housing rents: 120.4.** Twenty points above average and the highest component
  by a wide margin.
- **Goods: 93.8.** Groceries, clothing and cars cost less than the national average.
- **Utilities: 82.0.** Electricity and water are among the cheaper parts of the
  budget.
- **Other services: 96.2.** Eating out, health care and personal services sit a
  little under average.

So is Austin expensive? For a renter, yes: housing is the component furthest from
average. For everything else, no. A buyer or renter who can hold housing costs down
— a smaller place, a longer commute, a roommate — lives in a city that is otherwise
cheaper than the national average.

HUD's rent figures make the same point by bedroom count. The average rent in Austin
at HUD's 40th-percentile benchmark runs from $1,474 for a studio to $2,760 for four
bedrooms, all for Travis County and including the utilities a tenant pays.

## Texas income tax on your salary

There is none. Texas levies no personal income tax, so a single person on a $95,000
salary keeps about $75,663 after federal tax, Social Security and Medicare — an
effective 20.4%. That is the other half of the cost to live in Austin, and it is the
half that makes the rent easier to carry: the same salary in a state with an income
tax loses a few thousand dollars a year before rent is paid.

That is also why the salary Austin takes is modest. Covering the one-person month
above after federal tax and FICA takes a gross salary of about $52,344. The family of
four needs about $99,389, filing jointly. Both are break-even figures: they cover the
categories listed and leave nothing for savings.

## Who Austin suits

- **Households that need space but not much of it per person.** The step from one
  bedroom to two is small here; a couple or a family gets more for each extra dollar
  of rent than a single renter does.
- **Anyone moving from a taxed state on the same salary.** The paycheck gain is real
  and immediate, and it arrives before the rent comparison even starts.
- **People whose spending leans on goods and utilities rather than housing** — car
  owners, households with children, anyone who spends more on groceries than on
  square footage.

It suits least the single renter who wants to live alone close to the centre: that
person pays the city's highest-priced component in full and gets none of the
family discount.

## How we calculate this

Rent is HUD's Fair Market Rent for fiscal 2026 for Travis County — gross rent at the
40th percentile of recent movers, checked against HUD's own county file. Every other
line is the Bureau of Labor Statistics' 2024 average for a household of that size, carried to
current prices with each category's own Consumer Price Index, and then
multiplied by the matching BEA price level for Austin: goods for groceries, clothing
and cars; other services for eating out, health care, phone and insurance. Housing
and utilities are not counted twice, because HUD's figure already includes the
utilities a tenant pays. The salary comes from the same tax engine as the Texas
paycheck calculator.
