Two numbers decide whether a move, a job offer or a raise leaves you better off: what
the place costs, and what you keep there after tax. Most sites answer one of them.
The calculators here answer both, from official sources, for every state and every
US county and city.

## What a place costs

The [cost of living calculator](/tools/cost-of-living-calculator) builds a month for
your household in any US city, county or New England town: rent from the Department
of Housing and Urban Development's Fair Market Rents, every other line from what
households of your size spend, at that place's prices from the Bureau of Economic
Analysis. The [cost of living comparison](/tools/cost-of-living-comparison) sets two
places side by side, and the [cost of living by city](/cost-of-living) table ranks
every metro area on the same official index.

## What you keep

The [paycheck calculator](/tools/paycheck-calculator) takes a salary and a state and
shows what reaches your account, per check and per year, after federal income tax,
Social Security, Medicare and that state's tax. The [state income tax
rates](/state-taxes) page shows how each state taxes wages, and the [income tax
calculator](/tools/income-tax-calculator) works out the federal and state bill for the
year.

## Start here

Start with the question you actually have. If you are weighing a city, begin with the
cost of living and compare it with where you live now. If you are weighing an offer,
begin with the paycheck — what that salary leaves in that state, per check and per
year — and then set it against what the place costs. If you are renting or buying, the
rent and home affordability calculators show what your income carries before you look
at listings.

Every figure is calculated rather than estimated: tax from the published rules for the
year shown, prices from the Bureau of Economic Analysis, rents from the Department of
Housing and Urban Development. The methodology page explains each calculation and what
it leaves out.
