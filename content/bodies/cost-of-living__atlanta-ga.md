Atlanta is almost exactly average. BEA puts the metro at 100.1 against a national 100
— a tenth of a point from the middle of the country — across Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb,
Gwinnett and more than two dozen other counties holding over six million people. The
**Atlanta cost of living** has one line above normal, housing, and three at or just
below it.

## Fulton County rent, Atlanta prices

A single renter at HUD's one-bedroom benchmark of $1,660, buying what a typical
one-person household buys at Atlanta prices, spends about $3,855 a month — 43% of it
on rent, $46,259 over a year. Two adults and two children in an $1,820 two-bedroom
spend about $7,169, only a quarter of it on housing.

## Four indices, one of them high

- **Housing rents: 111.0.** Eleven points above average — the only component that is.
- **Goods: 100.4.** Groceries, clothing and cars at the national level.
- **Utilities: 96.2.** A little below.
- **Other services: 96.7.** Restaurants, health care and personal services, slightly
  cheaper than average.

Beside Houston, at 104.5, Atlanta's rent is a little higher; beside Tampa, at 125.8,
or Miami, at 155.6, it is modest. For a household deciding whether **Atlanta is
expensive**, the answer turns almost entirely on how much space it needs.

## One rent across the core counties

HUD applies the same Fair Market Rent to Fulton, DeKalb, Cobb and Gwinnett counties, so
the **average rent in Atlanta** on its benchmark is one set of figures for all four:
$1,585 for a studio, $1,660 for one bedroom, $1,820 for two and $2,605 for four. A move
from an intown apartment to the northern suburbs therefore changes the rent on the
benchmark very little — what it changes is the car lines. Atlanta is built around the
car, and a longer commute shows up in fuel, maintenance and time rather than in rent.

## Georgia tax on an Atlanta salary

Georgia taxes income at a flat 4.99% for 2026, cut from 5.19% during the year and
applied back to January. It allows a $15,000 standard deduction for a single filer,
$30,000 for a married couple, and $5,000 for each dependent. On a $95,000 salary a
single filer pays about $3,992 of state tax and keeps about $71,671 — an effective
24.6%. Atlanta levies no city income tax, so that figure holds across the metro.

## Where Atlanta pays off

- **Families.** The two-bedroom costs little more than the one-bedroom, Georgia's
  deductions for a married couple and for children are generous by flat-tax standards,
  and the non-housing prices are average.
- **People who drive anyway.** The metro rewards a car owner who would have had one
  regardless; it penalises one who is forced into a second.
- **Movers from the Northeast on a similar salary.** Rent, utilities and services all
  fall, and there is no city tax to replace them.

It suits least the person who wants to live without a car. The budget above assumes
one, as most Atlanta households have, and outside a few intown neighbourhoods the
city's layout makes going without a real constraint rather than a budget choice.
