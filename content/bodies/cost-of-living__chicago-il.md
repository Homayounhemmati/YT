Chicago is a big-city economy with a mid-sized city's rent. The Chicago metro area's
prices sit 3.6 points above the national average overall, and housing rents 12.0 points
above it — high for the Midwest, modest next to the coastal cities it competes with for
jobs. What surprises people who move here is the other direction: utilities are among
the cheapest of any large metro, at 83.6 on the federal index.

## Cook County rent, Chicago prices

Cook County's one-bedroom benchmark of $1,581 plus ordinary spending at Chicago prices
puts one person's month near $3,887, 41% of it rent; twelve such months cost $46,649.
Four people in a two-bedroom spend about $7,407 monthly, only 24% on housing, because
the $1,781 two-bedroom benchmark sits so close to the one-bedroom.

## Goods cost more than rent suggests

The component that stands out is not housing. Goods — groceries, clothing, cars,
household items — stand at 107.3, above the national average and above Houston's or
Atlanta's. Part of that is price and part of it is tax: the Chicago area's combined
sales tax is one of the highest in the country, and BEA's price levels are measured
with it included. For a household that spends more on things than on space, that is the
line to watch.

Other services, from restaurants to health care, sit at 100.5, almost exactly average.

Where Chicago bills arrive outside the index is property tax. Cook County's rates are
among the steepest of any big-city county, and an owner meets them directly; a renter
meets them inside the rent. The benchmark rents above already carry that cost, which is
one reason they sit above Midwestern norms.

## Against the coasts, and against the Midwest

Is Chicago expensive? Against New York, plainly not: a one-bedroom Fair Market Rent of $1,581 against New
York's $2,655, and utilities at 83.6 against 127.0. Against the rest of the Midwest,
yes, mostly because of rent. The useful answer is that **Chicago living expenses** are
about average for a large American city overall, with a cheaper-than-usual bill for
heating and power and a dearer one for everything bought at a register.

**Average rent in Chicago** barely separates by size at the lower end — $1,480 for a
studio, $1,581 with a bedroom, $1,781 with two — before jumping to $2,294 for three.

## A flat 4.95%, and no city levy

Springfield takes 4.95% of wages after a $2,925 exemption, and City Hall takes nothing:
Chicago has no income tax of its own. On $95,000 that leaves about $71,105, with $4,558
going to Illinois — identical in the Loop, Evanston, Naperville or Joliet. New York,
Philadelphia and Detroit residents cannot say the same; each of those cities adds a
wage tax on top.

## Who comes out ahead here

- **People moving from the coasts on a similar salary.** The rent gap alone is worth
  more than a thousand dollars a month against New York, and there is no city tax to
  give some of it back.
- **Renters who need space.** The step from one bedroom to two is small, so couples and
  families get more for each extra dollar of rent.
- **Households that spend on housing more than on goods.** The price levels reward the
  renter and charge the shopper.

It suits least the household that drives a lot and buys a lot — cars and goods are the
lines where Chicago runs above average, and the sales tax lands on both.
