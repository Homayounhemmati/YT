Annual figures are how salaries are quoted and monthly figures are how life is
paid for, but neither is what actually arrives. What arrives is a paycheck, and its
size depends on a pay frequency nobody negotiated.

## How a paycheck is calculated

The annual calculation is divided by the number of pay periods, then withholding is
applied to that slice:

| Frequency | Periods a year | $95,000 gross becomes |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | 52 | $1,827 |
| **Biweekly** | **26** | **$3,654** |
| Semi-monthly | 24 | $3,958 |
| Monthly | 12 | $7,917 |

**Biweekly and semi-monthly are not the same thing**, and the difference catches
people out. Biweekly is every two weeks — 26 paychecks, and two months a year
contain three of them. Semi-monthly is twice a month — 24 paychecks, always on the
same dates, always the same size.

A **biweekly paycheck calculator** therefore produces a smaller per-check figure
than a semi-monthly one at identical annual pay, and the two extra checks are what
make up the difference.

## What comes out of each check

```
gross per period
  − federal withholding    (from your W-4, not from your bracket)
  − state withholding
  − Social Security 6.2%   (until the wage base is reached)
  − Medicare 1.45%
  − pre-tax deductions     (401k, health premiums, HSA)
  = net pay
```

**Withholding is not the same as tax owed.** It is an estimate your employer makes
from the W-4 on file, and the annual return reconciles the two. A refund means too
much was withheld; a bill means too little was. Neither changes the tax — only when
it was paid.

Two things make checks differ in size across a year. The Social Security wage base
means high earners see their deduction stop partway through, so later checks are
larger. And bonuses are withheld at a flat supplemental rate that usually differs
from your marginal rate, which is why a bonus check rarely matches expectations in
either direction.

## What this does not include

- Local income tax, levied in nine states.
- Garnishments, union dues and post-tax deductions.
- Employer contributions, which are real compensation and never appear on the
  payslip.
- Irregular pay — commission, overtime and shift differentials.

## Who needs this

Anyone budgeting against what actually lands rather than against an annual figure,
and anyone starting a job where the pay frequency differs from their last one.
Moving from semi-monthly to biweekly changes every check you receive while changing
nothing about your salary, and the first month is where that becomes obvious.

A **payroll tax calculator** answers what arrives. If the answer is consistently
too low or too high, the fix is the W-4 rather than the budget.
