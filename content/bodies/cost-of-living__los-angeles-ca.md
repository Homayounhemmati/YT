Los Angeles sits just behind San Francisco on almost every line. BEA's price level for
the Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim metro area is 113.6, housing rents stand at 170.4 and
utilities at 158.6 against a national 100 — and a single renter's month comes to about
$4,695, half of it rent. The **Los Angeles cost of living** is a housing story first and
a driving story second.

## Los Angeles County, one renter and four

A one-bedroom at HUD's Los Angeles County benchmark costs $2,328, and with everyday
spending at LA prices a single person's year comes to $56,336. Rent's share, 50%, is
just under San Francisco's and just under New York's. A couple with two children in a
$2,903 two-bedroom spends about $8,665 a month, 34% of it on rent.

The ladder runs from $2,079 for a studio to $4,098 for four bedrooms. Orange County,
inside the same metro area and on the same price level, has a separate and higher
benchmark, while the Inland Empire counties to the east form their own metro with
cheaper rents — which is why so much of Southern California's workforce commutes
inland-to-coast every morning.

## A city built around the car

The budget's car lines — purchase and fuel, then insurance and repairs — are a national
household's driving priced at LA levels, and for most Angelenos they are not optional.
Distances are long, public transport covers the basin unevenly, and California's fuel
prices run well above the national average. A household that can live near work, or
near the rail lines, changes its budget more by that choice than by shopping around for
anything else.

**Is Los Angeles expensive?** For housing, power and anything with an engine, yes. Goods
at 106.6 and other services at 104.4 are only modestly above average, so a household
that spends most of its money on things rather than on space finds the city less extreme
than its rents suggest.

## California tax, and SDI on every check

A $95,000 salary in Los Angeles keeps about $69,982 after federal tax, Social Security,
Medicare, $4,446 of California income tax and $1,235 of State Disability Insurance, with
taxes alone an effective 25%. The city of Los Angeles levies no personal income tax, so
the figure is the same in Santa Monica, Pasadena or Long Beach.

Break-even for the one-person month is about $72,353 a year before tax; for a family of
four on one income, about $123,449. Both sit below San Francisco's equivalents and above
almost everywhere else on this site.

## Who Los Angeles favours

- **Two-earner households.** California's joint brackets are twice as wide as the
  single ones, and a second bedroom costs far less than a second home.
- **People whose work and home are close together.** The commute is the cost no price
  index captures well, and it is the one a household controls.
- **Movers from San Francisco.** Rent falls by several hundred dollars at every
  bedroom count and the paycheck is identical, since the state tax and SDI are the same.

It favours least the solo renter who drives across the county to work: half the month
goes to rent, and the car lines take much of what is left.
