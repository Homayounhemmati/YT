California has the highest top income tax rate in the country — 12.3%, plus a 1%
Behavioral Health Services Tax on income above $1,000,000 — but on an ordinary salary
the line that surprises people is a different one. Every California paycheck also
carries State Disability Insurance at 1.3% of all wages, with no cap, and it comes out
before the income tax is even counted.

## $95,000 in California

A single filer subtracts the **$5,900 standard deduction**, runs the rest through the
brackets and then takes a **$158 exemption credit** off the tax itself. The result is
**$4,446** of state income tax, about 4.7% of the salary, and SDI adds **$1,235**.

After $12,070 of federal income tax and $7,268 of Social Security and Medicare,
take-home is **$69,982**: taxes take an effective 25% of gross and SDI another 1.3%, the
lowest result of any state whose figures are checked on this site. Per check that is
about $2,692 every two weeks, $2,916 twice a month, $5,832 monthly or $1,346 a week.

## Nine brackets, and the one that matters

The schedule climbs from 1% through 2%, 4%, 6% and 8% to 9.3%, which for a single filer
starts at $75,197 of taxable income and runs all the way to $384,109. That long 9.3%
band is where most professional salaries sit: a raise, a bonus or overtime is taxed at
9.3% by the state, before SDI, for nearly anyone earning between the mid-seventies and
the high three hundred thousands. For a married couple filing jointly the band starts at
$150,394, so a two-earner household reaches it much later.

The thresholds move every year. California indexes its brackets, standard deduction and
credits to the state's own consumer price index, and for 2026 that adjustment was 3.4%.
A **california income tax calculator** still running last year's schedule overstates the
tax slightly on every bracket.

## Credits, not exemptions

Where most states let you subtract a personal exemption from income, California
subtracts a credit from the tax: $158 for each filer and $491 for each dependent. A
credit is worth the same whatever bracket the income reaches, which makes it worth more
to a lower earner than an exemption of the same size would be.

The credits shrink at high incomes. Above $260,778 of federal adjusted gross income for
a single filer ($521,561 on a joint return), each credit loses $6 for every $2,500 of
income, and a single filer's credit is gone a little above three hundred and thirty
thousand dollars.

## SDI: the uncapped payroll line

State Disability Insurance funds California's disability benefits and Paid Family
Leave, and since 2024 it has applied to every dollar of wages. Before that it stopped at
a wage ceiling, so a high earner paid a fixed amount; now someone on five times the
salary pays five times the SDI. It is withheld by the employer alongside income tax, and it is the main
reason a **california take home pay calculator** that models income tax alone comes out
too high.

Self-employment income is outside SDI unless the business owner elects coverage. That
reverses the usual result: the same $95,000 as self-employment profit leaves $70,338,
slightly more than the salary does, because the self-employed person pays both halves of
FICA but no SDI, and half of the self-employment tax lowers the income California taxes.

## Local tax, forms and filing

No California city or county levies a personal income tax, so a paycheck in San
Francisco is taxed the same as one in Fresno; what differs between them is rent.
Withholding runs on Form DE 4, administered by the Employment Development Department,
and the annual return is Form 540, filed with the Franchise Tax Board — two agencies,
one for what comes out of each check and one for the year's final bill.
