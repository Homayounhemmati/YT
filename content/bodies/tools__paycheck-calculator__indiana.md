Indiana's state income tax is a flat 2.95% for 2026, one of the lowest flat rates in
the country — but no Indiana resident pays only that. Every one of the state's 92
counties adds an income tax of its own, collected on the same paycheck, so the real
rate on an Indiana salary depends on which county you live in on the first day of the
year.

## $95,000 in Indiana, before the county

A single filer subtracts a $1,000 personal exemption and pays 2.95% on the rest:
**$2,773** of state tax, about 2.9% of the salary. After $12,070 of federal income
tax and $7,268 of Social Security and Medicare, take-home is **$72,890**, an effective
23.3%.

Per check that is about $2,803 every two weeks, $3,037 twice a month, $6,074 monthly
or $1,402 a week. Those are the figures before the county line, which is the part an
**indiana paycheck tax calculator** most often leaves out.

## The county line

County rates run from 0.5% in Porter County to 3% in Randolph County, and they apply
to the same base as the state tax — wages less exemptions. A few of the large ones:

| County | Main cities | 2026 rate |
|---|---|---|
| Marion | Indianapolis | 2.02% |
| Allen | Fort Wayne | 1.59% |
| Lake | Gary, Hammond | 1.5% |
| Hamilton | Carmel, Fishers | 1.1% |

In Marion County the county tax on the $95,000 salary is about **$1,899**, which is
more than two-thirds of the state tax again. Living in Hamilton County instead cuts
that roughly in half, and the difference is the same whether the job is in downtown
Indianapolis or at home: what counts is the county you live in at the start of the
year, not the county where the office is. Rates can change during the year, and the
Department of Revenue publishes each county's current rate in its Departmental Notice #1.

Someone who lives outside Indiana but works here pays county tax at the rate of the
county where the work is done, which is how the tax reaches commuters from Illinois.
Residents of Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin are covered by
reciprocal agreements instead and pay tax on those wages to their home state.

## Exemptions: small, but they add up for families

Indiana's personal exemption is $1,000 per person — you, a spouse and each dependent —
and each dependent child brings a further $1,500, so a child is worth two and a half
times what an adult is. A couple with two children subtracts seven times what a single
filer does before the rate applies, and the county applies its rate to the same
reduced figure.

There is no standard deduction. That is what keeps the effective rate close to the
headline one: almost every dollar of wages is in the base.

## Form WH-4 and filing

Withholding runs on Indiana's own certificate, Form WH-4, which asks for exemptions and
the county of residence and of work — the second answer is what sets the county line on
the stub. Returns go to the Indiana Department of Revenue on Form IT-40, with the county
tax calculated on the same return.

## Salaried and hourly

An **indiana salary calculator** and an hourly one run the same arithmetic on different
gross pay. With a flat state rate and a flat county rate, overtime is taxed at exactly
the same combined rate as the base hours, so the marginal cost of an extra shift is
simple to state: 2.95% to the state, the county's rate, the federal bracket and 7.65%
for Social Security and Medicare.

## Self-employed

The same $95,000 as self-employment profit leaves $71,585 after federal, state and
self-employment tax, with a state share of $2,575. The county tax applies on top, at the
same rate as for an employee.
