What a place costs depends on who is living there. One person in a studio and a
family of four in a three-bedroom home do not face the same city, so this calculator
starts from your household — how many people, how many bedrooms — and builds the
month line by line, saying where every line comes from. It covers every county in the
United States, every New England town and every incorporated city, not a short list
of big metros, which makes it a **cost of living calculator by city** in the literal
sense: type the place, get its month. A neighbourhood or unincorporated community is
found through its county.

## How cost of living is calculated

Rent is taken directly from the Department of Housing and Urban Development's Fair
Market Rent for the bedrooms you need, in the county — or, in New England, the town —
you choose. It is a gross rent, so it already includes the utilities a tenant pays,
and it is the one line published in dollars for every county in the country.

Every other line starts from what households of your size spend nationally, from the
Bureau of Labor Statistics' Consumer Expenditure Survey, carried forward to current
prices with each category's own Consumer Price Index, and then priced at the local
price level from the Bureau of Economic Analysis's Regional Price Parities. Goods are
priced with the goods index and services with the services index, because the two
move differently between places:

| Line | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Rent and utilities | HUD Fair Market Rent, by bedroom count |
| Groceries, clothing, car purchase and fuel, household supplies, furniture and household items | National spending × local goods prices |
| Eating out, health care and insurance, car insurance and repairs, public transport and fares, phone service, household services, education | National spending × local services prices |
| Entertainment, personal care | National spending × the overall local price level |

A price index of 100 is the national average; 112 means prices 12% above it, and 91
means 9% below. A place outside any metropolitan area takes the price level of its
state's non-metropolitan areas, which is how the Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes
it.

## Your figures replace the estimate

The line-by-line total is an estimate for a typical household of your size, and it is
labelled as one. Replace any line with what you actually spend — your rent, your car
payment, your grocery bill — and the total becomes yours. A household spending 45% of
its budget on rent and one spending 25% live in the same city very differently, and a
**living expenses calculator** that cannot take your numbers describes neither.

## From a monthly cost to a salary

The total is what a month costs after tax, so the calculator then works backwards to
the salary that pays for it: the gross pay whose take-home, after federal income tax,
Social Security, Medicare and that state's income tax and payroll contributions,
covers the year exactly. That answers **how much do i need to live in** a place in
the form a job offer is written in. Tell it how many adults and how many children
live there: two adults file jointly, a single parent as head of household, and every
child under 17 brings the federal child tax credit and whatever allowance the state
gives for dependents. You can change the filing status yourself.

Tax figures are checked state by state against each state's own publications. Where a
state's figures have not yet been checked, the salary is still shown, marked as such,
with the reason.

## Compared with where you live now

Add your current place and salary, and the result shows the same household's month
there beside the month here, and the salary here that keeps your spending power equal
after both states' taxes — next to the price-only answer, so you can see how much of
the difference is tax. A **cost of living estimator** that leaves tax out gets moves
between a taxed state and an untaxed one wrong by thousands of dollars a year.

## What this does not include

- Savings, debt repayment, retirement contributions and child care, which vary too
  much between households for an average to mean anything. The salary is a
  break-even figure before any of them.
- One-off moving costs, which are real but do not recur.
- Local income taxes, such as New York City's, which are named where they apply but
  not added.
- Variation within a county: the rent and price level are averages across
  neighbourhoods that can differ from each other more than two metros differ.

The price data is published annually and with a lag, so the year of every source is
shown next to the result.

## Who needs this

Anyone deciding whether they can afford a place before they move there — a new job, a
remote role, a return home. The question is rarely "is Austin expensive"; it is "does
$95,000 in Austin leave me better off than $85,000 where I am". A **monthly cost of
living calculator** that also solves for the salary and compares against home answers
that question in one place.
