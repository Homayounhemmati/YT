Maryland taxes income twice: once at the state's graduated rates and again through a
county income tax that every one of its counties, and Baltimore City, levies on its
residents. The county layer is not optional and not small, and a Maryland paycheck
figure that leaves it out overstates what you keep. The figures here cover the state
layer exactly and say plainly that the county layer is on top.

## The state layer on $95,000

Maryland starts from your wages, then takes off two things before its brackets
apply: a flat **$3,350 standard deduction** for a single filer, and a **$3,200
personal exemption**. What is left is taxed through these brackets:

| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| up to $1,000 | 2% |
| $1,000 to $2,000 | 3% |
| $2,000 to $3,000 | 4% |
| $3,000 to $100,000 | 4.75% |
| $100,000 to $125,000 | 5% |
| $125,000 to $150,000 | 5.25% |
| $150,000 to $250,000 | 5.5% |
| higher bands | 5.75% to 6.5% |

On a $95,000 salary the state tax comes to **$4,149**, and take-home after federal
tax, Social Security and Medicare is **$71,514** — an effective 24.7% before county
tax. Every two weeks that is about $2,751 a check.

## The exemption shrinks as income rises

Maryland's personal exemption is not fixed. Above $100,000 of federal adjusted gross
income it drops to $1,600, above $125,000 to $800, and above $150,000 it disappears.
A raise that crosses one of those lines costs slightly more state tax than the rate
alone suggests, and a **maryland income tax calculator** that uses a flat exemption
at every income will get higher salaries wrong.

## County tax

Each county sets its own rate within a range the state allows, and it is collected on
the same return and through the same withholding as the state tax. Because it varies
by county and applies to every resident, it is left out of the figures above rather
than guessed. Treat them as the most you could keep; the county line comes off that.

## Hourly pay and overtime

Hourly workers in Maryland pay the same layers on whatever their hours come to, with
overtime above 40 hours a week at one and a half times the regular rate. A **maryland
hourly paycheck calculator** has to apply the county tax to overtime as well — it is a
tax on all wages, not only the base.

## Self-employed

The same $95,000 as self-employment profit leaves $70,330 after federal, state and
self-employment tax, again before county tax.
