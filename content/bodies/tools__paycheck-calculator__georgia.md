Georgia replaced its graduated income tax with a single flat rate and has cut that
rate since. For 2026 it is **4.99%**, taken after a **$15,000 standard deduction** for
a single filer. The personal exemption for the filer is gone — it was folded into the
larger standard deduction when the flat tax began — and Georgia has no local income
tax.

## $95,000 in Georgia

Salary less the standard deduction, taxed at 4.99%: **$3,992** of state tax. After
federal tax, Social Security and Medicare a single filer keeps **$71,671**, an
effective 24.6%.

Every two weeks that is about $2,757 a check; twice a month, about $2,986; monthly,
about $5,973.

## The standard deduction does the heavy lifting

With a flat rate, the $15,000 deduction is what separates a Georgia worker on a modest
wage from one on a high salary. The first $15,000 of wages is untaxed by the state, so
the effective state rate on $95,000 is about 4.2%, not 4.99%, and lower still on
smaller salaries. A **georgia take home pay calculator** that skips the deduction
overstates the state line by several hundred dollars on this salary.

## Future cuts are conditional

Georgia's law schedules further reductions toward a lower flat rate, but each step
depends on the state meeting revenue conditions in the prior year. Whether a cut
happens is not known until it is announced, so this calculator uses only the rate
enacted for 2026 and does not assume the next one.

## Atlanta and the suburbs

Unlike New York City or Philadelphia, Atlanta levies no income tax on wages, and
neither does any Georgia county. What varies across the metro is property tax and the
local sales tax — which land on the cost of living, not on the paycheck.

## Across the state line

Two of Georgia's neighbours, Florida and Tennessee, tax no wages at all. For someone
weighing a job in Jacksonville or Chattanooga against one in Savannah or Atlanta, the
paycheck difference is the entire Georgia line — $3,992 a year on this salary — and it
runs in Florida's and Tennessee's favour before housing or sales tax are counted. In
the other direction, Alabama and South Carolina both levy graduated income taxes, so
the comparison there turns on the exact salary rather than on the headline rates.

## Dependents

Georgia still allows an exemption for each dependent, which this calculation, set for
a single filer with no dependents, does not apply. A **georgia paycheck tax
calculator** set up for a household with children will show a lower state tax.

## The Georgia withholding form

Federal withholding follows the W-4; Georgia withholding follows its own certificate,
Form G-4, which asks for marital status and allowances separately. Filling in the
federal form and not the Georgia one leaves your employer withholding at the default,
which may not match the tax this page computes. If the two differ, the gap comes back
as a refund or a bill when you file.

2026 has a second gap built in. The cut to 4.99% was signed in the spring and made
retroactive to January 1, but employers kept withholding at the old 5.19% until
mid-May 2026. Paychecks from the first months of the year therefore took slightly more
than the tax that is finally owed, and most Georgia employees will get that difference
back as a refund when they file.
