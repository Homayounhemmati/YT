Most comparison tools answer half the question. They tell you the cost difference
between two cities and stop — which leaves out the state tax difference, and that
can be larger than the rent gap it just showed you.

## How a cost of living comparison is calculated

Two figures are computed and then combined:

```
cost difference = destination cost − origin cost        (BEA index ratios)
tax difference  = origin net pay  − destination net pay (tax engine)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
real annual difference = tax difference − cost difference
```

The first is the standard comparison. The second is what almost nothing else
includes, and on a $95,000 income the spread between the best and worst US
jurisdiction is **$7,167 a year** — 7.5% of gross, from state tax alone.

That is the size of the number being left out of a **cost of living comparison
calculator** that stops at rent and groceries.

## The case that makes the point

Seattle and Portland are three hours apart, with broadly similar housing costs.
Washington levies no state income tax; Oregon's is among the highest in the
country. A straightforward cost comparison between them shows a modest difference.
The complete comparison shows several thousand dollars a year, in the direction
the cost comparison did not indicate.

That is not an edge case. It is what happens whenever two cities sit on opposite
sides of a state line, which is exactly the comparison people run most often.

## Two modes

**Same income, two places** — the relocation question. Your salary stays the same
and you want to know where it goes further.

**Two incomes, two places** — the job offer question. $120,000 in San Francisco
against $95,000 in Austin is not answered by either figure alone, and the ranking
frequently reverses once cost and tax are both applied.

## What this does not include

- Health insurance, which has no reliable metro-level index.
- Local income tax, levied in nine states — New York City and every Maryland
  county are the largest cases, and both move the answer materially.
- One-off moving costs.
- Neighbourhood variation inside a metro, which can exceed the difference between
  two metros.

Comparing across regions is also out of scope on purpose. BEA and Eurostat use
different bases, so a US-to-Europe comparison built from both would be arithmetic
without meaning.

## Who needs this

Anyone deciding between two places, and specifically anyone who has already looked
up rent in both and thinks that settles it. **To compare cost of living between
cities** properly you need the tax layer, and it is the layer most likely to
reverse the conclusion you arrived at from housing costs alone.
