Detroit is one of the few large American metros where housing costs less than the
national average. BEA's index of housing rents for the Detroit metro area stands at
94.7, below the national 100, while utilities stand at 107.0 and other services at
102.6 — the reverse of the pattern in most big cities, where rent is the expensive line
and everything else is ordinary. The **Detroit cost of living** is cheap where most
places are dear, and ordinary where most places are cheap.

## Wayne County rent, Detroit prices

Detroit's month for a single renter comes to about $3,366, or $40,392 a year, and only
a third of it — $1,122 at HUD's one-bedroom benchmark for Wayne County — is rent; in New
York rent is more than half. Four people in a $1,411 two-bedroom spend about $6,867,
just 21% on housing.

That is the key to reading Detroit's numbers: because rent is such a small share, the
other lines, which cost about what they cost anywhere, make up most of the budget. A
move to Detroit saves a great deal on rent and very little on everything else.

The price picture flips the usual big-city rule. The lines a household can shop
around — rent, and goods at 98.8 — are cheap, while those set by a utility or a
provider, power and water at 107.0 and services at 102.6, cost more than average.

## Rents across the metro

HUD applies one Fair Market Rent to Wayne, Oakland and Macomb counties: $1,009 for a
studio, $1,122 for one bedroom, $1,411 for two and $1,868 for four, including the
utilities a tenant pays. The benchmark therefore does not separate the city from its
suburbs, and neighbourhood matters more than county. **Average rent in Detroit** on
this measure is less than half of New York's for the same apartment, and below
Houston's $1,323 as well.

## Michigan tax, and Detroit's city tax

Michigan taxes wages at a flat 4.25% after an exemption of $5,900 per person. A single
person on $95,000 keeps about $71,876 after federal tax, Social Security, Medicare and
$3,787 of state tax — an effective 24.3%.

Detroit then levies its own income tax — the highest city rate in Michigan — on
everyone who lives in the city and, at half the rate, on non-residents who work there.
It is withheld from the same paycheck and it is not included in any figure on this page.
A household living in Ferndale or Royal Oak and working downtown pays the non-resident
rate; one living in the city pays the full rate wherever the job is. For a Detroit
resident, the city tax gives back a meaningful part of the rent saving.

That is why Detroit's break-even salary sits near the bottom of any large-metro list,
before the city's own levy lifts it for residents.

## Who Detroit's numbers favour

- **Renters and buyers whose budget is dominated by housing.** The saving is
  concentrated exactly there.
- **People who can live in the suburbs and still reach city jobs.** They keep the low
  rent and pay only the non-resident city rate, or none if the job is outside the city.
- **Remote workers on salaries set elsewhere.** Low rent against a national salary is
  the most favourable combination in these figures.

It suits least a household whose costs are mostly not housing — a family with high
health-care or car costs gains little from the rent level, and car insurance in
Michigan has long been among the most expensive in the country.
