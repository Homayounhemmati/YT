Tampa offers the same Florida paycheck as Miami at a much smaller rent premium. Housing
rents in the Tampa metro area stand at 125.8 on BEA's index, well above the national
100 but far below Miami's 155.6 — and every other component is below average: goods at
95.5, utilities at 88.5, other services at 97.6. Overall the **Tampa cost of living**
comes out at 100.9, less than a point above the middle of the country.

## Tampa living expenses, month by month

A one-bedroom at Hillsborough County's benchmark is $1,696, and one person's month at
Tampa prices adds up to about $3,857.

Rent takes 44% of it and the year comes to $46,287. A couple with two children in a
two-bedroom spends about $7,233 a month, 27% of it on rent.

Set the two Florida cities side by side and the difference is almost all housing. The
one-bedroom benchmark is $1,696 in Hillsborough County against $1,995 in Miami-Dade; the
two-bedroom is $1,977 against $2,436. The lines for food, health care and everyday goods
differ by a few dollars each.

## Tampa and St. Petersburg share a rent

HUD sets the same Fair Market Rent for Hillsborough and Pinellas counties — $1,593 for
a studio up to $3,077 for four bedrooms, including tenant-paid utilities — so on the
benchmark, living in St. Petersburg or Clearwater costs the same as living in Tampa
itself. The choice between the two sides of the bay comes down to the commute, the
neighbourhood and, for owners, flood zones and insurance rather than to rent.

## What the low utilities index means

At 88.5, Tampa's utilities index is among the lowest of any large metro. Electricity is
most of it, and Florida's per-unit prices are low by national standards. The budget above
does not apply that index a second time: HUD's rent figure already includes the
utilities a tenant typically pays, so they sit inside the first line. What the index
tells a newcomer is that a household running its air conditioning hard all summer uses
more power than the national average but pays less for each unit of it than in most of
the country.

## No state income tax

Florida levies no personal income tax and allows no local one, so a single person on
$95,000 keeps about $75,663 after federal tax, Social Security and Medicare — an
effective 20.4%, identical to Miami and to every other Florida city. Covering the
one-person month above takes a gross salary of about $54,894. The family of four needs
about $97,117 on one income filing jointly, after the child tax credit.

Florida recovers the revenue through sales and property tax, and homeowners across the
state have seen insurance premiums climb steeply. Renters meet those costs indirectly,
inside the rent; owners meet them directly, and they belong in any comparison with a
state that taxes income instead.

## Who Tampa suits

- **People choosing Florida for the tax, not for Miami.** They keep the full paycheck
  advantage and give far less of it back in rent.
- **Retirees and remote workers on fixed or portable incomes.** Every non-housing
  component is below average, so a budget that is not dominated by rent stretches
  further here than the overall index suggests.
- **Families.** The two-bedroom costs little more than the one-bedroom, and the family
  break-even salary is well under Miami's.

It suits least a buyer in a flood-prone area, where insurance and elevation requirements
can outweigh everything the rent and tax figures on this page describe.
