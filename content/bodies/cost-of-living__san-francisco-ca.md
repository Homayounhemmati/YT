San Francisco has the most expensive housing of any large metro in the country. BEA's
index of housing rents for the San Francisco–Oakland area stands at 194.7, almost
double the national 100, and utilities are not far behind at 172.6. The **San
Francisco cost of living** is 115.6 overall — less extreme than those two numbers
suggest, because goods (108.5) and services (106.2) are priced much closer to normal.

## A month on the peninsula

HUD's one-bedroom benchmark for San Francisco County is $2,977, and a single renter
spending like a typical one-person household at Bay Area prices goes through about
$5,385 a month, $64,623 a year. Rent is 55% of that: more than half, and a larger share
than in New York.

A family of four in a two-bedroom spends about $9,467 a month, and rent drops to 38%.
At $3,604, the second bedroom costs comparatively little, while food, health care and
transport roughly double with the household.

## The bedroom ladder

The benchmark climbs unevenly. A studio is $2,485; one bedroom adds a large step to
$2,977; three bedrooms reach $4,604, and four only $4,772. The cheapest unit here costs
more than a one-bedroom in Miami or Chicago. The **average rent in San Francisco** on
HUD's measure is the same for San Mateo and Marin, which share San Francisco's rent
area; the East Bay counties, Alameda and Contra Costa, have a lower benchmark of their
own, and that difference, rather than any price index, is what sends so many Bay Area
households across the bridge.

## Power, water and the rest

At 172.6, utilities are one of the highest components of any big-city price level.
Because HUD's rent figure already includes what a tenant typically pays for them, the
budget above does not add the index a second time — but anyone whose lease excludes
utilities should expect bills far above a national average. Goods at 108.5 reflect
California's sales tax as much as shelf prices; services at 106.2 are the closest
thing to ordinary in the city's budget.

## California tax and SDI

California taxes a $95,000 salary at $4,446 after its $5,900 standard deduction and $158
exemption credit, and State Disability Insurance takes another $1,235 at 1.3% of all
wages. Take-home is about $69,982 — taxes an effective 25%, SDI on top — the lowest of
the states whose figures are checked on this site. San Francisco adds no income tax of
its own; the city taxes businesses on their receipts, not residents on their wages.

So the salary a San Francisco month takes is high on both counts. Covering the
one-person budget after federal tax, FICA, state tax and SDI needs about $86,031; the
family of four, on one income filing jointly, about $137,409.

## Who it works for

- **Salaries set by the Bay Area's own labour market.** The prices assume a local
  wage; an offer at a national rate does not cover a local rent.
- **Shared households.** Rent is the line that scales least with people, so two
  earners in a two-bedroom carry it far more easily than one earner in a studio.
- **Commuters willing to cross the bay.** The East Bay benchmark, on the same metro
  price level, is the most direct saving available without leaving the region.

It works least for a single renter on an ordinary salary in the city itself, who hands
over more than half of each month before buying anything else — and, with SDI, gives a
larger share of each paycheck to the state than in any other state on this site.
