Virginia's income tax has four brackets, and the top one — 5.75% — starts at just
$17,000 of taxable income. That threshold was set in 1990 and has never been
adjusted for inflation, so a schedule that was once genuinely graduated now behaves
like a flat 5.75% tax on almost every full-time salary, with a small discount on the
first few thousand dollars.

## $95,000 in Virginia

A single filer takes the **$8,750 standard deduction** and one **$930 personal
exemption**, and the schedule taxes what remains: 2% on the first $3,000, 3% up to
$5,000, 5% up to $17,000 and 5.75% on everything above. On this salary that is
**$4,648** of state tax, about 4.9% of the salary.

After $12,070 of federal income tax and $7,268 of Social Security and Medicare, the
take-home is **$71,014**, an effective 25.2%. Per check that is about $2,731 every two
weeks, $2,959 twice a month or $5,918 monthly.

Among the states whose figures are checked here, that is the largest state income tax
bill on this salary — larger than New York's before New York City adds its own.

## Deductions that are fixed in law, not indexed

The standard deduction is written into the Code of Virginia as a dollar amount for a
range of years: $8,750 for a single filer and $17,500 for a married couple filing
jointly in 2025 and 2026, rising to $9,200 and $18,400 in 2027. The personal
exemption has stayed at $930 for years and is claimed for yourself, a spouse and each
dependent alike, so a couple with two children subtracts four of them. A **virginia
income tax calculator** that uses the federal standard deduction instead of the
state's much smaller one understates the bill by several hundred dollars.

Virginia also has no head-of-household status. A single parent who files as head of
household on the federal return files as single here, with the single standard
deduction, and claims the children as exemptions.

## Working in D.C. or Maryland

No Virginia city or county levies an income tax, which keeps the paycheck the same in
Arlington as in Roanoke. What changes the picture for Northern Virginia is where the
job is. A Virginia resident who works in Washington, D.C. pays income tax to Virginia
alone, because the District does not tax the wages of people who live outside it. A
Virginia resident who works in Maryland also pays Virginia only, under the reciprocal
agreement between the two states — Maryland's county taxes included.

So a household choosing between Fairfax and Montgomery County on the same salary is
choosing between Virginia's single state line and Maryland's state-plus-county one,
whichever side of the river the office sits.

## Form VA-4 and filing

Virginia withholding runs on its own certificate, Form VA-4, which counts exemptions
rather than using the federal W-4's steps. Returns go to Virginia Tax on Form 760, and
they are due on the first of May rather than in mid-April — one of the few states with
a later deadline than the federal one.

## Salary, hourly and overtime

A **virginia salary calculator** and an hourly one differ only in how gross pay is
built. Because the top bracket arrives so early, overtime and raises are taxed at
5.75% at the state level for practically everyone, which makes the marginal cost of
extra hours easy to predict: 5.75% to Virginia plus the federal bracket and 7.65% for
Social Security and Medicare.

## Self-employed

The same $95,000 as self-employment profit leaves $69,897 after federal, Virginia and
self-employment tax. The state takes less than on a salary — $4,262 — because half of
the self-employment tax comes off federal adjusted gross income, where Virginia's
calculation starts.
