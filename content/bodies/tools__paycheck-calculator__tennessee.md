Tennessee does not just go without an income tax on wages — its constitution forbids
one. Since a 2014 amendment, the legislature may not levy or authorise any state or
local tax on payroll or earned personal income, so no city, county or future
legislature can add a line to a Tennessee paycheck without a statewide vote to change
the constitution first.

## $95,000 in Tennessee, line by line

A single filer with no pre-tax deductions pays **$12,070** of federal income tax and
**$7,268** of Social Security and Medicare, and nothing else. Take-home is
**$75,663**, an effective 20.4%, which is as high as it gets anywhere in the country on
this salary.

Per check that is about $2,910 every two weeks, $3,153 twice a month, $6,305 on a
monthly payroll or $1,455 a week. A **tennessee take home pay calculator** has fewer
moving parts than most for exactly this reason: once the federal W-4 is right, every
figure on the stub is federal.

## The tax that used to reach investment income

Wages were never the target. Until recently Tennessee had the Hall income tax, which
applied only to interest and dividends, and a household with a savings account or a
brokerage account could owe it while its salary went untouched. The legislature
phased it down over several years and repealed it outright for tax years from 2021.
Nothing in Tennessee now taxes a resident's income of any kind, earned or invested.

That matters for anyone comparing states on more than the paycheck. Texas and Florida
also leave wages alone, and both write it into their constitutions too. Tennessee's
wording names payroll and earned income directly, and it binds local governments as
well as the state.

## What replaces it

The revenue comes from sales tax, and it shows at the register. Tennessee's combined
state and local sales tax rates sit among the highest in the country, and the state
taxes groceries, at a reduced rate, where many states exempt them. A household that
spends most of what it earns meets that tax on almost every purchase; one that saves a
large share of its income meets it far less. Property tax, by contrast, is comparatively
light for a state that goes without an income tax.

A small group of licensed professionals — lawyers and physicians among them — also
owe a flat annual professional privilege tax. It is billed to the individual once a
year, not withheld, so it never appears on a pay stub even though it comes out of the
same income.

## Moving here from a neighbouring state

Tennessee borders eight states, and every one of them taxes wages. In Virginia the same
$95,000 salary loses $4,648 to the state; in Georgia, Kentucky or North Carolina the gap
is several thousand dollars a year as well. For someone relocating to Nashville,
Memphis or Chattanooga on an unchanged salary, that is the first and most certain part
of the move's arithmetic — the rent comparison comes after it and can easily outweigh
it.

There is no state withholding certificate to fill in on arrival and no state return to
file for wages alone.

## Hourly pay and overtime

A **tennessee hourly paycheck calculator** needs the rate, the hours and the federal W-4,
nothing more. Overtime is time and a half above 40 hours in a week under federal law,
and it is withheld federally at whatever the larger check annualises to; there is no
state bracket for extra hours to push into.

## Working for yourself

Self-employment changes only the federal side. The same $95,000 as self-employment
profit carries $20,840 of tax, because a self-employed person pays both halves of
Social Security and Medicare, and leaves $74,160. Tennessee adds nothing to either
figure for a sole proprietor, although businesses organised as companies or
partnerships file the state's franchise and excise taxes instead.
